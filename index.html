01 · Core engineering work

smartCMS

Built through traditional software engineering, before vibe coding

A full-stack content and email operations platform for complex publishing workflows. It connected content selection, audience rules, reusable templates, campaigns, testing, scheduling, and operational data.

ReactTypeScriptRedux SagaGoMariaDBRedisDocker

The problem behind the code

Editors and technical teams were working across many dependent steps. A small change in content, templates, audience settings, or timing could affect everything downstream. The real work was understanding those relationships and turning them into a dependable system.

content & data→workflow rules→audience delivery

What I built

  • A React and TypeScript administration interface with reusable, metadata-driven tables and editing tools.
  • Newsletter builders for assembling articles, reusable templates, and audience-specific versions.
  • Campaign configuration for audience groups, A/B tests, RSS updates, and scheduled processing.
  • A Go API with JWT authentication, database access, email generation, background scheduling, and Redis-supported services.
  • A Docker-based environment connecting the web app, API, MariaDB, and supporting services.

How I approached it

I traced how the work moved between people and systems, modeled the underlying data and rules, and tested how decisions in one area affected later stages. The platform evolved through production use, edge cases, and changing requirements.

Why it matters

smartCMS best represents the depth of my engineering experience. I designed, implemented, debugged, integrated, and maintained the system through hands-on software engineering before AI-assisted vibe coding became part of development work.

How I think
Good systems begin with patient observation.

I enjoy the part before the answer is obvious: collecting clues, finding patterns, separating assumptions from evidence, and understanding why a problem keeps happening.

01

Observe the real workflow

I start with what people actually do, including workarounds, repeated friction, and the steps that are easy to overlook.

02

Map the relationships

I break a large problem into data, decisions, dependencies, and human needs, then look for the points where they influence one another.

03

Build and test carefully

I prefer small working versions, concrete feedback, and steady refinement over confident guesses.

04

Keep learning from the result

A finished feature still has something to teach. I watch how it behaves in practice and carry those lessons into the next design.
